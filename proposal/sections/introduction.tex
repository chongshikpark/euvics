\section{Introduction}

\subsection{EUV Light Source Design Criteria}

EUVICS is an effort to design and study a compact \EUV{} source based on
\ICS{}. The project aim is to determine whether a coordinated accelerator,
laser, interaction region, photon-transport system, diagnostics, and controls
can satisfy an eventually approved application need. This aim is not a claim
that the source has already achieved a specified wavelength, photon flux,
bandwidth, brilliance, efficiency, stability, readiness level, or experimental
result.

The application-level wavelength, bandwidth, flux, stability, polarization,
repetition-rate, and availability requirements for EUVICS are not yet approved.
They are tracked as candidate requirements SYS-EUV-001 through SYS-EUV-008 in
\texttt{tables/requirements\_matrix.csv}. The 13.5~nm case is retained as a
candidate design target rather than an application requirement. NIST maintains
calibration capability in the extreme ultraviolet, illustrating the need for
traceable radiometry when source performance is eventually
verified~\cite{NISTEUVDetectorCalibration}.

\subsection{Accelerator-Based EUV Light Sources}

In an inverse Compton source, a laser photon collides with a relativistic
electron. In a useful intuitive picture, the incident photon is Doppler
blueshifted into the electron rest frame, scatters there, and is boosted again
when transformed back to the laboratory frame. The higher-energy scattered
photons are concentrated near the electron's forward direction, but their
energy and angular distribution depend on the electron beam, laser pulse,
collision and observation geometry, polarization, recoil and nonlinear model,
and collection acceptance~\cite{SunWu2011ComptonSource}. Those dependencies do
not by themselves establish EUVICS performance or feasibility.

The source chain begins with electron production, acceleration, transport, and
focusing. A synchronized laser pulse is transported and focused to overlap the
electron bunch at the interaction point. Scattered photons are collected in a
defined forward acceptance and then spectrally selected, transported, and
measured, while residual electron and laser beams require controlled transport
or disposal. The interaction geometry, timing, transverse alignment, and pulse
overlap determine whether favorable single-particle kinematics produce a useful
source.

\subsection{ICS EUV Light Source Design Concept and Performance Goal}

For electron kinetic energy \electronKineticEnergy{}, the Lorentz factor is
$\gamma=1+\electronKineticEnergy/\electronRestEnergy$. In the weak-field,
recoil-free, head-on, on-axis limit, the familiar scaling is
\[
  E_s\simeq4\gamma^2E_L,
  \qquad
  \lambda_s\simeq\frac{\lambda_L}{4\gamma^2},
\]
where $E_L$ and $E_s$ are the incident and scattered photon energies and
$\lambda_L$ and $\lambda_s$ are their vacuum wavelengths. This is an
orientation-level limiting approximation, not the general EUVICS model. Exact
linear recoil, nonlinear laser strength, polarization, harmonic number,
off-axis observation, finite beam distributions, and detector acceptance must
be treated with their stated assumptions~\cite{pyEUVICSConventions050,pyEUVICSEquations050}.

The nominal roadmap inputs are a 3~MeV electron \emph{kinetic} energy and an
800~nm incident-laser vacuum wavelength. They are assumed inputs with approval
pending, not measured operating points. The 6.7~nm and 13.5~nm values are
candidate design targets, not predictions or measurements. A central-wavelength
calculation alone does not predict photon yield, bandwidth, brilliance, optical
throughput, or detected signal.

pyEUVICS is the reproducible calculation and validation layer for the
project-specific model. Version 0.5.0 at the locked source commit documents
linear and nonlinear kinematics, approximate spectrum and yield models, optical
transport, detector response, end-to-end workflows, and benchmark campaigns.
Its stated limitations remain part of the evidence: nonlinear harmonic
locations are not relative harmonic yields; the 6.7~nm CAIN comparison has
incomplete provenance and a known disagreement; the 13.5~nm campaign remains
provisional; and synthetic detector calibration demonstrates workflow plumbing
rather than measured calibration~\cite{pyEUVICSModels050,pyEUVICSValidation050}.
