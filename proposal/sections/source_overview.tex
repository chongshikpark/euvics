\section{Overview of Inverse Compton Scattering EUV Light Source}

\subsection{Physics of Inverse Compton Scattering}

Three regimes must remain distinct. Linear Thomson scattering neglects photon
recoil and laser-driven effective-mass effects. Exact linear Compton kinematics
retains recoil for single-photon scattering. Nonlinear ICS accounts for the
electron motion in a finite-strength laser field and may shift harmonic
locations or broaden spectral features. Pulsed-field geometry and nonlinear
motion are treated in the literature~\cite{KrafftEtAl2005NonlinearThomson,HartemannWu2013Brightness};
results from different regimes cannot be combined without matching their
assumptions.

Spectral position depends on electron kinetic energy, incident-laser wavelength,
collision angle, observation angle, recoil, normalized laser strength $a_0$,
polarization, and harmonic number where applicable. Yield and detected signal
also depend on bunch charge, laser photon number, spatial and temporal overlap,
beam sizes and pulse durations, crossing-angle reduction, repetition rate,
collection aperture, optical throughput, and detector response. Bandwidth and
angular structure additionally depend on electron energy spread and divergence,
laser bandwidth and intensity distribution, nonlinear broadening, aperture, and
correlations. Every reported width must identify its RMS, FWHM, or $1/e^2$
convention.

\subsection{Accelerator System}

The candidate accelerator boundary includes an electron source, injector,
energy-setting system, transport, focusing, steering, diagnostics, and dump. It
must deliver a characterized electron bunch to the interaction point and safely
transport the residual beam afterward. Kinetic energy and spread, bunch charge,
transverse size and divergence, emittance, bunch duration, arrival time,
position, and pointing all couple to source output. The selected technology,
numerical allocations, interfaces, and verification criteria remain TBD.
Published SRF photoinjector reviews and experiments describe other machines,
not EUVICS performance~\cite{ArnoldTeichert2011SRFPhotoinjectors,PetrushinaEtAl2020SRFGun}.

\subsection{Laser, Interaction Region, and Photon Diagnostics}

The candidate laser boundary includes pulse generation, transport, focusing,
polarization control, synchronization, diagnostics, and controlled residual-beam
transport. The interaction region must establish the collision geometry and
spatiotemporal overlap while providing electron, laser, and EUV diagnostics.
Downstream EUV collection, spectral selection, optics, and calibrated detectors
must preserve a traceable distinction between generated, accepted, transported,
and detected quantities. Wavelength, pulse energy, intensity-FWHM duration,
$1/e^2$ waist, one-RMS bandwidth, polarization, timing, stability, acceptance,
and throughput values remain TBD except for the roadmap's 800~nm nominal
assumed input. Laser-to-RF synchronization and time-stamping methods are
reviewed in the ultrafast-electron literature~\cite{FilippettoEtAl2022UED};
EUVICS-specific tolerances require an approved overlap and performance budget.
