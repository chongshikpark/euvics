\section{Overview of Inverse Compton Scattering EUV Light Source}

\subsection{Kinematics and Model Regimes}

Let \electronKineticEnergy{} be electron kinetic energy in an explicitly stated
energy unit. Electron total energy is
$\electronTotalEnergy=\electronKineticEnergy+\electronRestEnergy=\gamma\electronRestEnergy$,
with
\[
  \gamma=1+\frac{\electronKineticEnergy}{\electronRestEnergy},
  \qquad
  \beta=\frac{v}{c}=\frac{\sqrt{(\gamma-1)(\gamma+1)}}{\gamma}.
\]
Here $m_e$ is electron rest mass, $c$ is the vacuum speed of light, and $v$ is
electron speed. An accelerator value such as 3~MeV is interpreted as kinetic
energy only when labeled as such; it must not be substituted as total energy.

The collision angle \collisionAngle{} is measured between the electron and
incident-laser propagation vectors. An angle of $0^\circ$ is co-propagating;
$180^\circ$ is head-on. The observation angle \observationAngle{} is measured from the
electron propagation direction, so $\observationAngle=0$ is forward observation.
The current pyEUVICS linear model is coplanar and takes positive
\observationAngle{} toward the incident laser, making the angle between incident
and scattered photons $\collisionAngle-\observationAngle$.

For incident photon energy $E_L$ and scattered photon energy $E_s$, the exact
single-photon linear Compton relation implemented by pyEUVICS 0.5.0 is
\begin{equation}
  E_s=E_L
  \frac{1-\beta\cos\collisionAngle}
  {1-\beta\cos\observationAngle+
  \dfrac{E_L}{\gamma\electronRestEnergy}
  \left[1-\cos(\collisionAngle-\observationAngle)\right]}.
  \label{eq:exact-linear-ics}
\end{equation}
All angles in Eq.~\ref{eq:exact-linear-ics} are evaluated in radians. The
denominator's final term is photon recoil. Removing it gives the linear Thomson
approximation. For head-on, on-axis, recoil-free scattering,
\begin{equation}
  E_s=\gamma^2(1+\beta)^2E_L\simeq4\gamma^2E_L,
  \qquad
  \lambda_s\simeq\frac{\lambda_L}{4\gamma^2},
  \label{eq:ics-limiting-scaling}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_L$ and $\lambda_s$ are vacuum wavelengths. The final forms use
the relativistic limit and are scaling relations, not the general EUVICS
model~\cite{pyEUVICSConventions050,pyEUVICSEquations050}.

The applicable physics regimes are kept separate:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Linear Thomson:} weak field and recoil-free single-photon
        scattering. It is useful for limits and checks, not for silently
        replacing recoil-aware or finite-intensity calculations.
  \item \textbf{Exact linear Compton:} free-electron, single-photon scattering
        with recoil, represented by Eq.~\ref{eq:exact-linear-ics} for the current
        coplanar geometry.
  \item \textbf{Nonlinear ICS:} finite normalized laser strength $a_0$ modifies
        electron motion. The current pyEUVICS effective-mass model estimates
        central harmonic locations with explicit polarization, harmonic, and
        recoil settings. A calculated harmonic location is not a relative
        harmonic yield or a complete nonlinear spectrum.
\end{itemize}
Pulsed nonlinear Thomson treatments and ponderomotive broadening establish
additional dependencies~\cite{KrafftEtAl2005NonlinearThomson,HartemannWu2013Brightness};
they do not supply EUVICS output without matched inputs and assumptions.

\subsection{Controls on Generated and Detected Output}

The central spectral position depends on electron kinetic energy, laser vacuum
wavelength, collision angle, observation angle, recoil, $a_0$, polarization,
and harmonic number where applicable. It does not determine photon yield,
bandwidth, brilliance, or detected signal.

Yield per collision additionally depends on total electron bunch charge and
laser photon number; transverse and temporal overlap; electron and laser sizes;
pulse durations; offsets and timing jitter; crossing-angle reduction; and the
applicable cross section. Average rate or power also depends on repetition rate.
Accepted and detected quantities further depend on angular and spectral
acceptance, optical throughput, and wavelength-dependent detector response.
Generated, accepted, transported, and detected photon number, pulse energy,
peak power, and average power must remain separately named.

Bandwidth and angular distribution depend on electron kinetic-energy spread,
emittance and divergence, laser bandwidth and intensity distribution,
collision-angle and pointing jitter, nonlinear broadening, observation aperture,
and correlations. Under the pyEUVICS Gaussian conventions, electron spatial,
energy-spread, and divergence widths are one RMS; laser waist is the transverse
$1/e^2$ intensity radius; laser duration is intensity FWHM; and laser fractional
bandwidth is one RMS. Any alternative convention must be labeled and converted
before comparison. A reported spectral peak, centroid, RMS width, or
Gaussian-equivalent FWHM must identify its domain, aperture, weighting, and
statistical definition~\cite{pyEUVICSModels050}.

The current pyEUVICS yield model uses rigid Gaussian beams and a weak-scattering
luminosity model. Its optional hourglass factor is a screening estimate, its
angular acceptance is axisymmetric rather than a polarization-resolved
differential cross-section integration, and combined angular and wavelength
fractions currently assume independence. The sampled spectrum propagates
declared spreads and aperture through the central-location model; it is not a
complete nonlinear radiation spectrum and its samples are not yield weighted.
These qualifications must accompany results derived from those models.

\subsection{Subsystem and Interaction-Point Interfaces}

The accelerator must produce, accelerate, transport, focus, and diagnose the
electron bunch. Kinetic energy, bunch charge, normalized emittance, energy
spread, bunch duration, arrival time, transverse position, and pointing require
approved definitions and allocations. Transport must deliver the specified
phase space to the interaction point and carry the residual electron beam to a
controlled dump. The selected source, radio-frequency, lattice, focusing,
diagnostic, and dump designs remain TBD. Results from other SRF photoinjectors
or linacs are contextual evidence, not assigned EUVICS capability~\cite{ArnoldTeichert2011SRFPhotoinjectors,PetrushinaEtAl2020SRFGun,CiovatiEtAl2018LowEnergySRFLinac}.

The laser system must provide a characterized vacuum wavelength, pulse energy,
intensity-FWHM duration, $1/e^2$ waist, bandwidth, polarization, wavefront,
pointing, and timing at the interaction point. Peak intensity, pulse-average
intensity, and repetition-rate-average power are distinct. For the pyEUVICS
Gaussian pulse model, $a_0$ is derived from the peak electric-field amplitude;
the supplied pulse energy, waist, duration, wavelength, and declared $a_0$
therefore require a consistency check. Laser-to-radio-frequency synchronization,
transport loss, focus characterization, and residual laser handling remain TBD
design interfaces~\cite{FilippettoEtAl2022UED,pyEUVICSModels050}.

The interaction region must define the collision plane and angle, observation
axis and aperture, focal positions, timing reference, vacuum boundary, alignment
tolerances, and overlap diagnostics. A theoretical wavelength remains
insufficient if the two beams do not overlap with the assumed spatial and
temporal distributions. Diagnostics must distinguish electron, laser, and EUV
measurements and preserve calibration, uncertainty, and configuration provenance.

Forward EUV collection must define geometric acceptance before spectral
selection and transport. Every mirror, aperture, filter, grating, and detector
requires wavelength-dependent efficiency and provenance; incidence angles must
state whether measured from the surface normal or as grazing angle from the
surface plane. Optical loss, detector responsivity, electronics response, and
acquisition processing must remain distinct stages. A single nondispersive
photodiode measures integrated signal and cannot directly identify wavelength
without a dispersive element or independently constrained spectrum
~\cite{pyEUVICSInstrumentation050}.

\subsection{Role and Validation Boundary of pyEUVICS}

pyEUVICS is the reproducible calculation and validation layer, not an alternate
set of equations maintained in this document. The locked 0.5.0 documentation
covers exact linear kinematics, nonlinear effective-mass and harmonic-location
estimates, seeded spectrum sampling, Gaussian overlap and yield, energy and
power accounting, optical transport, detector and electronics response,
end-to-end workflows, and benchmark campaigns. Calculations must record the
package version, configuration, units, energy and angle conventions, model,
polarization, harmonic, recoil flag, aperture, random seed and sample count
where applicable, and input or calibration provenance.

The validation boundary remains explicit. The project-reported 6.7~nm CAIN
comparison lacks its input deck, code version, aperture, beam distributions, and
spectral-feature definition; the documented mismatch remains a known
disagreement and no empirical correction is justified. The 13.5~nm reference
campaign is provisional because no independent simulation or calibrated
hardware measurement has been supplied. Synthetic detector calibration verifies
workflow plumbing only and is not measured detector calibration. Physics review
must therefore distinguish numerical convergence and target matching from
independent validation~\cite{pyEUVICSValidation050}.
